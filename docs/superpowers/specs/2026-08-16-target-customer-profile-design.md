# Target customer profile: the Home-Ground Collector

**Date:** 2026-08-16 · **Status:** approved in session; written form pending Dom's read ·
**Scope:** governs every customer-facing surface (landing page, order flow, social
captions, email replies). The nerd channels (Show HN, the technical blog post) keep
their own register, per `docs/marketing.md` §Audiences.

## Why this document exists

The landing page says "a 2.6 pt trail is literally 2.6 pt on the glass," twice. Dom's
verdict: a customer doesn't care that a track is 2.6 pt wide. The line traces
upstream. The translation table in `docs/marketing.md` carries it in its
*customer-facing* column, which means the table itself drifted from customer language
into engineering proof, and fixing the page without fixing the understanding of the
customer would regrow the problem.

This document is that understanding: who the customer is, what they care about in
life, and how they want to be spoken to. It is the Tecopa Plateworks counterpart to
the Map-Holder in Friction (`00_Resources/brand-position.md`), the house standard for
what a usable profile looks like. The copy rework is a separate plan that executes
against this profile.

## Decisions made with Dom (2026-08-16)

| Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Center of the profile | One person: the home-ground devotee | Matches the five-plate reality, the price point, and the editions ritual. The other segments (runners, gift buyers, data nerds) become channels or purchasers, never co-equal personas. |
| Product focus | The physical poster | The target person wants a physical artifact of their outdoor life. Wallpapers and the film are supporting cast on customer surfaces. |

## The two real people behind the profile

The profile generalizes from two actual candidate customers Dom can name, not from
segments:

- **The shed hunter.** Hunts sheds in the Northeast California high desert and marks
  where he finds them. The poster he wants shows where the sheds have turned up, with
  the pattern visible: which country produces, which never has. Photos of found sheds
  pinned to the spots they came from.
- **The Tahoe peak-bagger.** Hiking all the peaks around Tahoe. The poster he wants
  shows every summit done so far, trails climbing from the lake's circle to the peaks
  that surround it. (Tahoe sits outside all five built plates, verified against
  region bounds 2026-08-16, so this person can only be served through the
  request/commission door. That funnel is real, with a named prospect waiting on it.)

What they share is the profile. Neither is an athlete by identity. Both are
**place-bound collectors**: they accumulate marks on one landscape across years
(sheds found, summits reached), and both keep score.

## Who they are

They live in, or within striking distance of, one landscape, and their weekends have
a pursuit with a tally: sheds, summits, bucks, lakes. They record tracks and drop
waypoints out of habit on onX, Gaia, or a watch. Marking spots is part of the
pursuit, so years of data already sits on their phone, never once seen as a whole.

The phone is a tool the way binoculars are a tool. They are not tech people and have
no wish to be. They spend real money where quality counts (glass, boots, a rifle) but
they price wall art like a normal person. Their walls are already spoken for:
antlers, a euro mount, family photos. The poster does not compete with the
collection. It is the map of where the collection came from.

## What they truly care about

- **Knowing their ground.** Better than anyone else does, across seasons and years.
  The poster proves the knowing.
- **The tally.** "23 of 30" and "so far" are how they think. The list is not
  finished, which is the honest way in for editions: the map fills in, year over
  year. (Never "the January 1st ritual." That framing belongs to a customer this
  profile retired.)
- **The marks more than the lines.** Sheds are points. Summits are points. Tracks
  are how they got there. The pins, the photos on the spots, the places earned:
  that is what they will point at. Copy that leads with trail ink is admiring the
  wrong layer.
- **The stories.** Every mark is one they would tell. The poster on the wall is the
  prompt: someone asks, they point, the story starts.
- **The people they show.** The tailgate buddy, the kid they will bring out. The
  poster hangs in the house and is seen by people they invited.
- **Proof in the hand.** Things that matter get printed, mounted, hung. A poster is
  trusted the way a screen never is.

## Their doubts, which the copy must answer

All craftsman-transaction questions, none of them technical:

1. **"Will it look good?"** Answered by showing: the renders, the 1:1 detail crop.
   Never by specification.
2. **"Is it a hassle?"** They do not know what an export is called in onX. Step one
   must be concrete to the tap.
3. **"Is $80 worth it?"** Anchored against what any framed print costs, and against
   the fact that nothing else on earth can be their years on their ground.
4. **"Will my stuff show up right?"** The one trust story that survives translation,
   told plainly: nothing prints until you have seen it and said yes.

Struck deliberately: "do you keep my tracks," "what if the company disappears," and
every file-architecture reassurance. Those answer questions this person never asked,
and asking them on his behalf plants the doubt. The stories stay true and stay
available in the nerd channels, where they are the pitch.

## How they want to be spoken to

Primary evidence, Dom's own tailgate pitch for the shed hunter, verbatim:

> "Hey, I've created this service where you can upload your GPX tracks and then print
> out a poster of everywhere you've gone to in a specific area. So you'd be able to
> see where you've found all your sheds, and you can even upload pictures of the
> found sheds, and they can get placed on the spot where you found them. The poster
> quality is super nice, and I worked on making the map look as great as possible."

Nothing from the press lexicon survived the tailgate: no plate, no edition, no proof,
no cartouche. The sentences are built as *you, plain verb, his pursuit*. The quality
claim is a maker vouching, with zero numbers. The rules, each derived from that
evidence:

1. **Second person, plain verbs.** Upload, see, print, hang, point. The pitch's
   shape is the target: "you'd be able to see where you've found all your sheds."
2. **Name the pursuit.** Sheds, summits, elk camp, the lake. The word "adventures"
   is a segment word; a pursuit word is respect. Dom said "sheds" three times in
   three sentences.
3. **The maker is present.** "I built this. I work on every sheet." One person
   standing behind the thing, which is also simply true: the press is a person.
4. **Quality is shown and vouched, never measured.** "Super nice" plus the detail
   crop outsells any specification. Customer-meaningful numbers only: 18×24, $80.
   No pt, dpi, ppi, hash, deterministic, byte-identical, or archival jargon.
5. **Press lexicon is seasoning, never load-bearing.** "Edition" and "plate" may
   flavor a sentence whose meaning stands without them. A first-time reader decodes
   nothing. "One score, three performances" fails this test.
6. **Sell the seeing.** The emotional core of the purchase is revelation of
   accumulation: years of data, never once seen as a whole, suddenly one picture.
   "You've never seen all of it in one place" is the itch the hero must scratch.
7. **New products reach them through a buddy, never a brand.** Seen on a wall, shown
   at a tailgate, posted in a pursuit group. The page's register is a buddy showing
   you a thing he made. No campaign voice, no subscription smell: one price, a
   person to email.

Illustrative translations (final wording belongs to the copy-rework plan):

| The page says today | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "The proof is the print — pixel-for-pixel, by design" | "You see the exact poster before it prints. Nothing ships until you say yes." |
| "A 2.6 pt trail is literally 2.6 pt on the glass" | Cut. If the wallpaper needs a line: "The wallpaper matches your print, line for line." |
| "One score, three performances" | Lead with the print; the wallpaper and film are "it comes with" lines, told in plain words. |
| "Plates are versioned and hash-addressed" | Cut from customer surfaces. The nerd channels keep it. |
| FAQ: "Do you keep my tracks?" | Drop the question. At most, one plain line at the order door about emailing your tracks to the person who makes your poster. |

## What this person does not care about

Structural filter, for the rework plan to cut against: determinism as a word,
manifests, schemas, licenses, hash addressing, physical units, typography talk,
"performances" of a "score," privacy reassurance, and any sentence about the file
format. None of these are false. All of them are the builder talking to the
reviewer instead of the buddy across the tailgate.

## Consequences for existing surfaces and docs

The copy-rework plan that follows this profile must touch:

1. **`marketing/landing.html`** — poster-first hierarchy (the print is the product;
   wallpaper and film support), the tailgate register throughout, the trust block
   rebuilt around the four doubts, the FAQ swapped to their questions, both 2.6 pt
   lines out, privacy material off the page.
2. **`docs/marketing.md`** — re-translate the translation table's right column
   against this profile; demote the four-audience list to channels around the one
   persona; extend the 2026-08-13 hierarchy correction with its second half: even
   craft copy is written from the customer's chair, not the builder's.
3. **`tests/test_marketing_page.py`** — the page's claims are pinned by tests;
   every copy change lands with its pins updated in the same change.
4. **Pricing display** — the $149/18×24 versus $80-sensibility tension is recorded
   here and decided by Dom, outside the copy rework.
5. **The request/commission door** — earns its place on the page (a named prospect
   sits outside every built plate) and gets rewritten in this register.

## Out of scope

- Writing the new copy (the next phase, via the writing-plans skill).
- Pricing changes (Dom's decision, informed by the recorded tension).
- Product or engine changes; the profile changes what is said, never what is built.
- The nerd-channel materials (Show HN post, technical writing): pillar 2 remains
  their lead, per the standing rule in `docs/marketing.md`.
